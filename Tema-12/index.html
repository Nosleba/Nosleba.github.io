Learn to order food and drinks in a restaurant or café. Use polite expressions, ask for the price, and interact in a roleplay.

Learning Objectives

  • 🍽️Learn menu vocabulary – main dishes, drinks, desserts.
  • 🗣️Use key structures: I want…, Can I have…?, How much is it?
  • 🎭Act out a restaurant roleplay – customer and waiter.

Why This Matters

Ordering food is one of the most practical skills in any language. You’ll use these phrases when travelling, eating out, or even ordering takeaway. This lesson gives you the confidence to communicate in real-life dining situations.

Key Structures

Three essential phrases for ordering food and asking about price.

1. I want… (I would like…)
StructureExampleSpanish
I want + nounI want pizza.Quiero pizza.
I want + noun phraseI want a glass of water.Quiero un vaso de agua.
I’d like (more polite)I’d like some rice.Me gustaría un poco de arroz.
Use: "I want" is direct. "I’d like" is more polite. In restaurants, "I’d like" is very common.
2. Can I have…? (polite request)
StructureExampleSpanish
Can I have + noun?Can I have the bill?¿Me puede traer la cuenta?
Can I have + noun phrase?Can I have a coffee, please?¿Me puede dar un café, por favor?
Use: "Can I have…?" is a polite way to ask for something. Always add "please" to be more courteous.
💰 3. How much is it? / How much are they?

🔹 Singular

How much is it? – ¿Cuánto cuesta?

Use for one item: a pizza, a drink, the spaghetti

👉 How much is the pizza? – It’s $10.

🔹 Plural

How much are they? – ¿Cuánto cuestan?

Use for multiple items: the pizzas, the drinks, the fries

👉 How much are the fries? – They’re $3.50.

🔹 Other useful expressions

How much does it cost? (alternative) How much is that? (pointing) What’s the price? (formal)

💬 Example dialogue

A “How much is the spaghetti?”
B “It’s $12.50.”
A “How much are the fries?”
B “They’re $3.50.”
📝 More useful expressions
  • I’d like to order… – I’d like to order a pizza.
  • For here or to go? – (waiter asks) – For here, please. / To go, please.
  • I’ll have… – I’ll have the chicken.
  • Could I have…? – more formal than "Can I".
  • The bill, please. – to ask for the check.

Dialogue – Restaurant Roleplay

Two conversations: one in a restaurant and one in a café. Practice both customer and waiter roles.

Dialogue 1 – In a restaurant

Roleplay · Customer and waiter
Waiter
"Good evening. Can I take your order?"
Customer
"Yes, I’d like the spaghetti and a salad, please."
I’d like = I would like
Waiter
"And something to drink?"
Customer
"Can I have a glass of red wine, please?"
Waiter
"Certainly. Anything else?"
Customer
"No, that’s all. How much is the spaghetti?"
Waiter
"It’s $14.50."

Dialogue 2 – At a café

Roleplay · Ordering coffee
Barista
"What can I get for you?"
Customer
"I want a latte, please."
Barista
"For here or to go?"
Customer
"To go, please. How much is it?"
Barista
"That’s $4.25."
Customer
"Here you go. Thank you!"
🔑 Key phrases for roleplay
  • →Can I take your order?
  • →I’d like / I want …
  • →Can I have … please?
  • →How much is it?
  • →For here or to go?
  • →The bill, please.
💡 Roleplay tip
When you are the waiter, remember to ask:
"Anything else?" or "Would you like dessert?"
When you are the customer, be polite: "Please" and "Thank you".

Pronunciation – Polite intonation

In English, polite questions and requests often have a rising intonation. This makes you sound friendly and courteous.

↗
Rising intonation for polite requests
Can I have…? Could I get…?
The pitch goes up at the end of the question. This makes it sound like a polite request, not a demand.
↘
Falling intonation for statements
I want pizza. How much is it?
Statements and wh-questions usually have falling intonation. For "How much is it?" the pitch falls at the end.
🎯 Practice these polite phrases
  • ↗ Can I have a glass of water, please?
  • ↗ Could I have the menu, please?
  • ↗ I’d like a coffee, please.
  • ↘ How much is the pizza?
  • ↘ I want some rice.
