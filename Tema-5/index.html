Colors & Adjectives

Learn to name colors and use adjectives to describe people and objects. Say "She is tall." and "He is funny." with confidence!

Learning Objectives

  • 🎨Name and recognize 10 colors in English: red, blue, green, yellow, orange, purple, pink, white, black, brown.
  • 📝Use adjectives to describe people and objects: big, small, tall, short, happy, sad, funny, kind, fast, slow.
  • 🗣️Build descriptions using the structure: "She is tall." / "He is funny." / "The ball is red."
  • 🔊Pronounce short vowel sounds correctly in: big /ɪ/, red /ɛ/, happy /æ/.

Why This Matters

Colors and adjectives are the building blocks of description. Once students know these words, they can describe anything they see — people, animals, objects, drawings — and make their conversations much richer and more expressive.

💡 How adjectives work in English
In English, adjectives come BEFORE the noun when describing directly (e.g. "a red ball") OR AFTER the verb to be when in a sentence (e.g. "The ball is red.").

In Spanish the order is sometimes reversed ("una pelota roja" vs "a red ball") — so the order is important to practice!

Grammar Focus

How to describe people and objects using verb to be + adjective.

Structure 1 — Describing people
Formula:
[Subject] + is/are + [adjective].
Examples: She is tall. He is funny. They are happy.
Use this structure when you describe a person — how they look or how they feel.
Structure 2 — Describing objects & colors
Formula:
The [noun] is + [color/adjective].
Examples: The ball is red. The cat is small. The house is big and blue.
Use this to describe any object — its color, size, or other qualities.

⚠ The golden rule — am / is / are
I → am (only I uses "am")
He / She / It → is (one person or thing)
You / We / They → are (you, groups, or multiple people)

✗ "They is happy." → ✓ "They are happy."
✗ "I is tall." → ✓ "I am tall."
⚠ Common mistake — adjective position
✗ "She is a tall girl tall."
✓ "She is a tall girl."
✓ "The girl is tall."

The adjective goes only ONE time — either before the noun or after "is". Not both!
✓ Combining two adjectives with "and"
You can describe something with two adjectives using "and":

"My sister is tall and funny."
"The box is big and red."

Keep it to two adjectives for now — that's enough at A1 level!

Dialogue

Two real conversations using colors and adjectives to describe people and objects.

Dialogue 1 — "Describing a person"

Dialogue · Describing people with adjectives
A
"Who is your best friend?"
B
"My friend is tall and funny."
adjective + adjective with "and"
A
"Is she tall or short?"
B
"She is tall. And she is very kind!"
verb to be + adjective
A
"What color is her backpack?"
B
"It is blue. A big blue backpack!"
color + adjective + noun

Dialogue 2 — "What color is it?"

Dialogue · Colors & objects
A
"Look at this cat! What color is it?"
B
"It is orange and white."
two colors with "and"
A
"Is it big or small?"
B
"It is small. But it is fast!"
A
"Is it happy?"
B
"Yes! It is very happy."
Key phrases in these dialogues
  • →She / He is [adjective].
  • →It is [color].
  • →What color is it / your [noun]?
  • →Is it big or small?
  • →[Adjective] + and + [adjective].
💡 Tip — "very" adds intensity
Add "very" before any adjective to make it stronger:

"She is tall." → "She is very tall."
"It is happy." → "It is very happy."

"Very" never changes — it stays the same for all adjectives!

Pronunciation

The key challenge: short vowel sounds — essential for saying big, red, and happy correctly.

/ɪ/
Short "i" — as in "big"
big pink him sit
This is a short, quick sound — like a short "i" in Spanish but even shorter.

Tip: Say "ee" (like in "sí") but stop halfway — very quick: "big" not "beeg".
✗ "beeg" → ✓ "bɪg"
/ɛ/
Short "e" — as in "red"
red yellow bed ten
This sounds like a short "e" — like the "e" in Spanish word "el".

Tip: Open your mouth slightly, say "e" quickly and clearly.
✗ "raid" → ✓ "rɛd"
/æ/
Short "a" — as in "happy"
happy black cat fast
This sound doesn't exist in Spanish — it's between "a" and "e".

Tip: Open your mouth wide, smile slightly, say "a" — like a surprised "a".
✗ "heppy" → ✓ "hæpi"
/ʌ/
Short "u" — as in "funny"
funny sun run blue — no!
This relaxed "u" is very central — like saying "a" with a relaxed mouth.

Tip: Relax your mouth completely, say "uh" quickly.
✗ "foony" → ✓ "fʌni"

Note: "blue" has a long /uː/ sound — different from "funny"!

Common mistakes — Spanish speakers

big
✗ "beeg"
✓ /bɪg/
Short "i" — don't stretch it out!
red
✗ "reid" / "rid"
✓ /rɛd/
Keep the "e" short and clear, ends with /d/.
happy
✗ "heppy" / "hapy"
✓ /ˈhæpi/
Wide mouth for the /æ/, double-p in the middle.
💡 Fun trick — "The Mirror Game"
Hold up a mirror (or use your phone camera!) and watch your mouth shape:

• big /ɪ/ — lips slightly apart, relaxed
• red /ɛ/ — mouth opens a little, corners neutral
• happy /æ/ — mouth opens wide, like a big smile!

Practice the sequence: big → red → happy — repeat 3 times and watch how your mouth changes each time!
