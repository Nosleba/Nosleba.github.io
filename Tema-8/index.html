Daily Routines

Learn to talk about your daily routine using the Present Simple affirmative. Describe what you do every day: wake up, eat breakfast, go to school, study, play, sleep.

Learning Objectives

  • ⏰Talk about your daily routine using Present Simple: I wake up at 7.
  • ➕Understand the difference between I/you/we/they (base form) and he/she/it (verb + -s).
  • 🗣️Describe your routine in a short spoken presentation.
  • 👂Listen and put daily activities in the correct order.
  • 🔊Pronounce connected speech naturally: wake_up, go_to.

Why This Matters

Talking about your daily routine is essential for basic communication. You use the Present Simple every time you describe what you do, what time you do it, and how often. This lesson gives you the exact vocabulary and grammar to speak about your day.

Go to the school = visit the building (not as a student). For daily routines, we say go to school.

Present Simple Affirmative

Two structures: I wake up at 7. (I/you/we/they) and She studies at night. (he/she/it + -s).

Structure 1 — I / You / We / They
Formula:
[I/You/We/They] + base verb
Examples: I wake up at 7. We go to school together. They play soccer on Saturdays.
Use this for all subjects except he/she/it. No extra -s.
Structure 2 — He / She / It
Formula:
[He/She/It] + verb + -s / -es / -ies
Examples: She studies at night. He plays guitar. My sister eats breakfast quickly.
For he, she, it you MUST add -s (or -es / -ies). This is the most common mistake for Spanish speakers.

Conjugation table with routine verbs

SubjectVerb formExampleSpanish
Ibase formI wake up at 7.Me despierto a las 7.
Youbase formYou eat breakfast.Tú desayunas.
Webase formWe go to school.Nosotros vamos a la escuela.
Theybase formThey play outside.Ellos juegan afuera.
He / She / Itverb + -s/-es/-iesShe studies. He sleeps. It works.Ella estudia. Él duerme. Funciona.
⚠ Important — Don't add -s to the pronoun!
✗ He play → ✓ He plays
✗ She go → ✓ She goes
The -s goes on the verb, not on "he" or "she". Remember: He + plays, not "He play".

Dialogue

Two real conversations about daily routines — see Present Simple in action.

Dialogue 1 — Morning routine

Dialogue · What's your morning routine?
A
"What time do you wake up?"
Question with "do" (we'll learn later)
B
"I wake up at 7 o'clock. Then I eat breakfast."
I + base verb
A
"Does your brother wake up early?"
B
"Yes, he wakes up at 6:30. He goes to school by bus."
He/She + verb-s (-es)

Dialogue 2 — After school

Dialogue · After school activities
A
"What do you do after school?"
B
"I study for one hour, then I play video games."
A
"And your sister? What does she do?"
B
"She studies too, but she sleeps early — at 9 PM."
Notice: she + studies / sleeps
Key phrases from these dialogues
  • →What time do you wake up?
  • →I wake up at 7. / I eat breakfast.
  • →He wakes up at 6:30.
  • →She studies / sleeps early.
  • →Does your brother…? → Yes, he does.
💡 Tip — "Do" vs "Does"
For questions: Do + I/you/we/they → Do you play?
Does + he/she/it → Does she study?
Remember that the main verb goes back to base form: Does he play? (not "plays").

Connected Speech

In natural English, words join together. Practice wake_up and go_to.

wake_up → /weɪkʌp/
Linking consonant to vowel
wake_upI wake_up at 7.
The /k/ sound at the end of "wake" connects directly to the /ʌ/ sound of "up". It sounds like one word: "wake_up". Don't put a pause between them.

go_to → /ɡoʊtə/
Weak form of "to"
go_toI go_to school.
The word "to" is pronounced very fast: /tə/. Together with "go", it sounds like "go_tuh". The /t/ links smoothly.

💡 Practice these full sentences — link the words!
  • 🔊 I wake_up at 7.
  • 🔊 I go_to school at 8.
  • 🔊 She wakes_up early. (wakes_up → /weɪksʌp/)
  • 🔊 He goes_to work by car. (goes_to → /ɡoʊztə/)
