Grammar Focus

How to describe family members using My + family member + verb to be + adjective.

Structure 1 — One adjective
Formula:
My [family member] is + [adjective].
Examples: My mother is kind. My father is tall. My sister is happy.
Structure 2 — Two adjectives with "and"
Formula:
My [member] is [adj] and [adj].
Examples: My brother is tall and funny. My grandmother is old and kind. My cousin is young and brave.

⚠ The golden rule — is vs are
One person (father, mother, brother…) → is
Two or more people (parents, grandparents…) → are

✗ "My parents is tall." → ✓ "My parents are tall."
✗ "My mother are kind." → ✓ "My mother is kind."
⚠ Common mistake — "My" vs "He/She"
✗ "My mother she is kind."
✓ "My mother is kind."
✓ "She is kind."

Don't say "my mother" AND "she" in the same sentence — pick one or the other!
✓ Negative: "is not" / "isn't"
You can also say what someone is NOT:

"My sister is not tall. She is short."
"My brother isn't sad. He is happy!"

"is not" = "isn't" — same meaning, shorter form

Dialogue

Two real conversations about family. Read, listen, and practice with a partner!

Dialogue 1 — "Tell me about your family"

Dialogue · Describing family members
A
"Do you have brothers or sisters?"
B
"Yes! I have one brother and one sister."
I have + number + family member
A
"What is your brother like?"
B
"My brother is tall and very funny!"
My [member] is [adj] and [adj]
A
"And your sister? Is she tall too?"
B
"No, she isn't tall. She is short and happy!"
negative + positive description

Dialogue 2 — "Your parents"

Dialogue · Parents and grandparents
A
"What is your mother like?"
B
"My mother is kind and very smart."
A
"Is your father tall?"
B
"Yes! He is very tall and strong."
Yes + pronoun + is/are + adjective
A
"Do you have grandparents?"
B
"Yes! My grandparents are old but very funny!"
"but" = contrast between adjectives
Key phrases in these dialogues
  • →"What is your [member] like?" — asking for a description
  • →"My [member] is [adj]." — simple description
  • →"He/She is [adj] and [adj]." — two adjectives
  • →"She isn't tall. She is short." — negative + correction
  • →"…old but very funny!" — "but" for contrast
💡 "but" vs "and"
Use "and" when both adjectives are similar:
"She is tall and strong."

Use "but" when there's a contrast or surprise:
"He is old but very fast!"
"She is small but very brave!"
🎭 Role Play Tip
Practice with a partner! One person plays Speaker A (asking questions) and the other plays Speaker B (answering about their REAL family).

Then switch roles. Make it personal — talk about YOUR family!

Pronunciation

The key challenge: the TH sound — essential for saying mother, father, and brother correctly.

/ð/
Voiced TH — "mother, father, brother"
mother father brother they
This TH is voiced — your throat vibrates!

How to do it: Put the tip of your tongue lightly between your teeth, then blow air and use your voice. It sounds like a buzzing "d".

✗ "moder" → ✓ "moðer"
✗ "fader" → ✓ "faðer"
/θ/
Unvoiced TH — bonus words
think three teeth with
This TH is unvoiced — no throat vibration, just breath.

How to do it: Same tongue position as /ð/, but just blow — no voice sound.

It sounds like a soft "s" or "f" but with the tongue out.
✗ "sink" → ✓ "θink"
/ər/
The "er" ending — mother, father, sister…
mother father sister brother
All these family words end in "er" — pronounced as a relaxed "er" sound.

Tip: Relax your tongue and say "uh" with a slight "r" curl. It's soft, not strong!

✗ "moder-A" (don't add a final vowel)
✓ "mo-ðər" (soft, quick ending)
Stress
Word stress in family words
MO-ther FA-ther SIS-ter GRAND-fa-ther
The stress is always on the first syllable for most family words.

Clap it out:
MO-ther → CLAP-clap
GRAND-fa-ther → CLAP-clap-clap
GRAND-mo-ther → CLAP-clap-clap

Common mistakes — Spanish speakers

mother
✗ "mo-der" / "mo-ther" (t sound)
✓ /ˈmʌðər/
Tongue between teeth, use your voice — no "d" or "t"!
father
✗ "fa-der" / "fa-ther" (t sound)
✓ /ˈfɑːðər/
Same TH as mother — voiced, tongue between teeth.
brother
✗ "bro-der" / "bro-ter"
✓ /ˈbrʌðər/
Note: the first vowel is /ʌ/ like "sun" — not "oh"!
💡 Fun trick — "The Tongue Game"
Practice the TH sound with this sequence:

1. Open your mouth and smile slightly.
2. Put the tip of your tongue gently between your upper and lower teeth — just barely touching!
3. Blow air and say "d" at the same time — that's /ð/ (voiced TH)!

Now practice: mother → father → brother — say it 3 times fast and feel your tongue touching your teeth each time!
