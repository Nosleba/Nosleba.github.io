Present Simple Basics

Learn to talk about daily routines and habits using the present simple tense. Compare "I am happy" (to be) with "I play soccer" (action verbs).

Learning Objectives

  • ⏰Talk about daily routines using present simple: I eat breakfast, I go to school.
  • 🔤Understand the difference between verb "to be" (I am happy) and action verbs (I play soccer).
  • ➕Add -s / -es / -ies for he, she, it: She plays, He studies, It watches, He goes.
  • 🎧Identify daily activities in listening tasks and short conversations.
  • 🗣️Pronounce final -s correctly in plays, drinks, watches, goes, brushes.

Why This Matters

The present simple is the most common tense in English. You use it every day to describe what you do, what you like, and what happens around you. Mastering it helps you speak fluently about your life.

Grammar Focus

Key difference: I am happy (to be) vs I play soccer (action). Conjugation rules for present simple.

Verb TO BE (state / feeling)
Examples:
I am happy.
She is a teacher.
They are tired.
We use TO BE for feelings, descriptions, and states — NOT for actions.
ACTION VERBS (do / routines)
Examples:
I play soccer / drink coffee / go to school.
She studies / watches TV / brushes teeth.
We eat dinner at 7.
Use action verbs to talk about activities and habits.

📘 Spelling rules (-s / -es / -ies)
• Most verbs: add -s (play→plays, drink→drinks)
• Verbs ending in -sh, -ch, -s, -x, -o: add -es (watch→watches, go→goes, brush→brushes)
• Verbs ending in consonant + y: change y to -ies (study→studies, cry→cries)
⚠ Important: don't use "to be" for actions
❌ I am play soccer → ✅ I play soccer
❌ She is go to school → ✅ She goes to school.
Remember: am/is/are is only for states, not for actions.

Pronunciation

Final -s in present simple has three different sounds: /s/ , /z/ , /ɪz/. Learn with all 10 verbs.

/s/
Voiceless sound
eatssleepsdrinksworks
After voiceless consonants: /p/, /t/, /k/, /f/. No vibration.
/z/
Voiced sound
playsreadsstudiesgoesbrusheswatches
After vowels and voiced consonants. Most common! (goes → /ɡoʊz/)
/ɪz/
Extra syllable
watchesbrushes
After -s, -sh, -ch, -x, -z. Adds a syllable: "watch-es" → /ˈwɒtʃ.ɪz/
🎯 Focus on our 10 verbs
plays, studies, goes, reads, brushes, watches → /z/ or /ɪz/ (brushes/watches = /ɪz/)
drinks, eats, sleeps, works → /s/ (voiceless final).
💡 TPR trick — Hand on throat
Place your hand on your throat. Say goes, plays → vibration. Say drinks → no vibration. Practice the difference!
🎧 Listen & repeat
Practice pairs: drink/drinks, go/goes, watch/watches, brush/brushes, work/works. Use the vocabulary cards for accurate audio models.
