Likes & Dislikes

Learn to express what you like and don't like, ask others about their preferences, and answer with short responses.

16Topic words
3Grammar structures

Learning Objectives

  • 👍Express likes using I like … and He/She likes …
  • 👎Express dislikes using I don’t like … and He/She doesn’t like …
  • ❓Ask questions with Do you like …? and Does he/she like …?
  • 🗣️Conduct interviews with classmates about their preferences.
  • 👂Listen and identify what people like and don’t like.

Why This Matters

Talking about likes and dislikes is essential for everyday conversations. You use these structures to make friends, order food, plan activities, and understand others. This lesson gives you the tools to express your preferences clearly and ask about other people's.

💡 Tip — "Like" + noun or verb-ing
We use like with a noun: I like pizza.

Grammar – Expressing Preferences

Affirmative, negative, and questions with like.

Affirmative & Negative
SubjectAffirmativeNegative
II like pizza.I don’t like fish.
YouYou like coffee.You don’t like tea.
He / She / ItShe likes music.She doesn’t like pop.
We / TheyThey like sports.They don’t like tennis.
Remember: with he, she, it we add -s in affirmative (likes), but in negative we use doesn’t + base form (like).
Questions & Short answers
QuestionShort answer (+)Short answer (−)
Do you like pizza?Yes, I do.No, I don’t.
Does she like music?Yes, she does.No, she doesn’t.
Do they like sports?Yes, they do.No, they don’t.
Notice: in questions with he/she/it we use does + base form (not likes).
📝 More examples from real contexts
  • • I like chocolate, but I don’t like ice cream.
  • • My brother likes video games, but he doesn’t like reading.
  • • Do you like swimming? – Yes, I do. I swim every weekend.
  • • Does your mother like cooking? – No, she doesn’t. She prefers eating out.
  • • We like travelling, but we don’t like flying.

Dialogue – Interviews

Two conversations where people ask about likes and dislikes.

Dialogue 1 – At a party

Dialogue · Getting to know someone
A
"Hi! Do you like music?"
B
"Yes, I do. I love pop music. What about you?"
A
"I like rock, but I don’t like classical."
I don’t like classical.
B
"Do you play an instrument?"
A
"No, I don’t. But I like singing."

Dialogue 2 – Class interview

Dialogue · Interviewing a classmate
A
"Does your brother like sports?"
B
"Yes, he does. He likes football and basketball."
He likes football.
A
"Does he like swimming?"
B
"No, he doesn’t. He doesn’t like water!"
A
"And you? Do you like swimming?"
B
"Yes, I do. I swim every weekend."
🔑 Key phrases from the dialogues
  • →Do you like…? – Yes, I do. / No, I don’t.
  • →Does he/she like…? – Yes, he/she does. / No, he/she doesn’t.
  • →I like / I don’t like
  • →He likes / doesn’t like
  • →What about you?
💡 Interview tip
When interviewing, ask follow-up questions:
"Do you like pizza?" → "What kind of pizza?"
"Does she like music?" → "What instrument does she play?"

Pronunciation – Question intonation

In English, yes/no questions usually have a rising intonation at the end.

↗
Rising intonation (yes/no questions)
Do you like pizza? Does she like music?
The pitch goes up at the end of the question. This signals that you are asking for confirmation.
↘
Falling intonation (wh-questions)
What do you like? What does she like?
Wh-questions (what, where, when, etc.) usually have a falling intonation. The pitch goes down at the end.
🎯 Practice these questions
  • ↗ Do you like swimming?
  • ↗ Does your father like cooking?
  • ↗ Do they like travelling?
  • ↘ What do you like to eat?
  • ↘ What music does she like?
