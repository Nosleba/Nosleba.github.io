Learning Objectives

  • 👤Identify and use the 7 personal pronouns: I, You, He, She, It, We, They.
  • 🔗Match pronouns with the correct form of to be: am, is, are.

Why This Matters

Pronouns and the verb to be are the skeleton of English. You cannot make a single sentence without them. Once you master this, every other grammar structure becomes easier to build.

⚠ Watch out — "It" in English
In Spanish, objects are masculine or feminine (el libro, la mesa). In English, all objects, animals, and things use It — gender does not exist for non-human things.

Example: "The cat is big." → "It is big." — not "He is big" or "She is big."
📌 "You" for both singular and plural
In English, You is used for both one person and many people — unlike Spanish, which has tú (singular) and ustedes / vosotros (plural). Context tells you which it is.

Pronunciation

Two contractions that are tricky for Spanish speakers. Getting these right makes your English sound fluent and natural.

I'm
Contraction of I am
I'm fine I'm 12 I'm happy
Pronounced as one smooth syllable: /aɪm/ — like the English word "aim".

Spanish speakers often say "I am" as two distinct words. In spoken English, always contract it to I'm — it sounds more natural and is actually easier once you practice.
He's / She's
Contractions of He is / She is
He's tall She's kind He's funny
He's = /hiːz/ and She's = /ʃiːz/ — both end with a buzzing /z/ sound.

Do not confuse He's (He is) with His (possession). They sound similar but mean different things: "He's happy" ≠ "His book."

They're vs There vs Their — Common confusion

They're
/ðeər/
Contraction of They are.
"They're students."
There
/ðeər/
A place or existential.
"There is a school."
Their
/ðeər/
Possession.
"Their teacher is nice."
⚠ All three sound exactly the same! /ðeər/
In speaking, they are identical. The difference only appears in writing. For now, focus on They're = They are — you'll use it the most at this level.

1
