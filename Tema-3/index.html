Why This Matters

⚠ "years old" — don't forget it!
In English, we always say "I am 8 years old" — not just "I am 8" in formal speech. In casual speaking, "I'm 8" is fine, but for class, use the full form.

Pronunciation

Two tricky letter distinctions for Spanish speakers: B vs V and E vs I. Getting these right will make your spelling much clearer.

B vs V
Two different letters — two different sounds
B — /biː/ V — /viː/
In Spanish, B and V sound almost identical. In English, they are clearly different:

B /biː/ — lips close together and pop open (like in ball, book)
V /viː/ — upper teeth touch the lower lip and vibrate (like in very, violin)

When spelling, this distinction is critical: "Is it B or V?"
E vs I
Long vowel sounds — very different
E — /iː/ I — /aɪ/
E in the alphabet = /iː/ — a long "ee" sound, like in see or tree.
I in the alphabet = /aɪ/ — sounds like the word "eye".

When spelling a name, "E" and "I" must be very clear:
"Is it E as in elephant or I as in ice cream?"

Alphabet letter names — Commonly confused letters

A / E / I
/eɪ/ · /iː/ · /aɪ/
These three vowels have very different names. Practice them slowly: ay — ee — eye.
G / J
/dʒiː/ · /dʒeɪ/
G sounds like "jee" and J like "jay". Easy to confuse when spelling fast.
M / N
/ɛm/ · /ɛn/
Both start with /ɛ/. Say M as "em" and N as "en" — hold the ending sound.
💡 Tip — Use a word to clarify
When spelling and someone can't hear a letter, use a word:
"B as in Ball" · "V as in Very" · "E as in Elephant" · "I as in Ice cream"
