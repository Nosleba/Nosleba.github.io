Learning Objectives

  • 💬Greet people using formal and informal expressions in English.
  • 🪪Introduce yourself — say your name and ask for someone else's.
  • 🔊Pronounce key sounds correctly: /h/ and the long vowel /aɪ/.

Why This Matters

Greetings are the first thing you say in any language. Knowing how to greet someone correctly — and in the right context — immediately makes your English sound more natural and confident.

📌 Key difference
Hello and Hi both mean hola, but Hello works in formal and informal situations, while Hi is only casual — you would not say "Hi" to your teacher on the first day. Good morning / Good afternoon are time-specific greetings and are always polite.

Grammar Focus

Four key structures using the verb to be. These are the building blocks of your first real conversation.

Core Structures

StructureUseSpanish
My name is ___.Introduce yourselfMi nombre es ___.
What is your name?Ask someone's name¿Cuál es tu nombre?
How are you?Ask how someone feels¿Cómo estás?
I am fine.Answer "How are you?"Estoy bien.
Verb to be — key rule

In all these structures, to be connects the subject with information about it:

My name is Ana.

I am fine.

What is your name?

Contraction — short form

In spoken English, I am is almost always shortened:

I am fine→I'm fine

Question vs Statement

Statement

My name is Carlos.

Subject + verb to be + information. Ends with a period.

Question

What is your name?

Question word + verb to be + subject. Ends with a question mark.

Dialogue

Full Dialogue — Meeting someone new

Dialogue · School Setting

Dialogue · Formal version (e.g. meeting a teacher)
A
"Good morning. What is your name?"
B
"Good morning. My name is Carlos. Nice to meet you."
A
"Nice to meet you too, Carlos."
Structure of the dialogue
  • 01Open with a greeting
  • 02Ask the other person's name
  • 03Give your own name
  • 04Ask "How are you?"
  • 05Answer the question
  • 06Close with "Nice to meet you"

Pronunciation

Two sounds that appear constantly in English greetings. Getting these right makes a big difference.

/h/
The H sound
HelloHiHow
Air passes gently through an open throat — like fogging a mirror.

The /h/ does not exist in Spanish the same way. Avoid the silent h of Spanish — in English it is always pronounced.
/aɪ/
The long I sound
HiIfinegoodbye
Start with an open /a/ sound (mouth wide), then close toward /i/ (mouth narrows). It's a gliding vowel — one sound in motion.

Think of it as the Spanish sound "ai" said quickly.

Minimal Pair Drill

These pairs differ by only one sound. Train your ear to hear the difference, then train your mouth to produce it.

HivsHe
/haɪ/ vs /hiː/ — different vowel after /h/
HivsBye
/haɪ/ vs /baɪ/ — only the first consonant changes
finevsfan
/faɪn/ vs /fæn/ — long vs short vowel

Exercise 2 — Choose the right greeting

Situation: You meet your teacher at 8 am.

(a) Hi! (b) Good morning (c) Goodbye

Situation: You meet a new classmate at lunch.

(a) Good morning (b) Hi! (c) Good afternoon

(a) Hello (b) How are you? (c) Goodbye
