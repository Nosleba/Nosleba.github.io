Learning Objectives

  • 👨‍👩‍👧‍👦Name and recognize 6 key family members: mother, father, brother, sister, grandmother, grandfather.
  • 🗣️Introduce family members using "This is my mother." and "I have a brother."
  • 👂Identify family relationships in listening activities and short descriptions.
  • 🔊Pronounce the TH sound correctly in: mother, father, brother.

Why This Matters

Family is one of the first topics children talk about in any language. Knowing these words lets students describe who they live with, who they love, and make real conversations with classmates.

⚠ Watch out! — "Grandmother" vs "Grandfather"

Grammar Focus

Two key structures to present and talk about family members: This is my… and I have a…

Structure 1 — Introducing someone
Formula:
This is my [family member].
Examples: This is my mother. This is my brother. This is my grandfather.
Use this structure when you point to a photo or person and want to say who they are.
Structure 2 — Talking about your family
Formula:
I have a [family member].
Examples: I have a sister. I have a grandfather. I have a brother and a sister.
Use this to describe your family — telling others who is in it.

Full structure comparison

StructureEnglishSpanishUse it when…
This is my… This is my father. Este es mi papá. Pointing to / introducing someone
I have a… I have a sister. Tengo una hermana. Describing your family in general
She / He is… She is my mother. Ella es mi mamá. Describing who someone is (from Tema 2)
⚠ Important — "a" vs "my"
"I have a sister" — use "a" when you say what you have in general.
"This is my sister" — use "my" to show it belongs to you.

Never mix them: ✗ "This is a my sister" → ✓ "This is my sister"

Key phrases in these dialogues
  • →This is my [family member].
  • →I have a [family member].
  • →His / Her name is…
  • →Do you have a brother / sister?
  • →Yes! / Yes, I do.
💡 Tip — His vs Her
His = for boys/men (father, brother, grandfather)
Her = for girls/women (mother, sister, grandmother)

"His name is Carlos." · "Her name is Sofia."

Pronunciation

The biggest challenge for Spanish speakers: the TH sound — essential for saying mother, father, and brother correctly.

TH — /ð/
The voiced "TH" sound — used in family words
mother father brother
In Spanish, there is no TH sound. Many students say "moder" or "fader" — this is incorrect.

How to make the TH /ð/ sound:
1. Put the tip of your tongue gently between your teeth.
2. Blow air while making your voice vibrate — like a soft buzzing "d".
3. Practice slowly: th… th… th… mo-th-er.

This is the voiced TH (voice vibrates). There's also a voiceless TH (like in "think") — but we focus on the voiced one for now.

Common mistakes — Spanish speakers

mother
✗ "moder" / "moser"
✓ /ˈmʌð.ər/
Tongue between teeth, not a D or S sound.
father
✗ "fader" / "faser"
✓ /ˈfɑː.ðər/
The F at the start is correct — only the TH needs work.
brother
✗ "broder" / "broser"
✓ /ˈbrʌð.ər/
The trickiest — BR blend + TH together. Go slow!
💡 Fun trick — "The Tongue Game"
Tell your students: "Stick your tongue out a tiny bit — like you're making a funny face — then say the word."

This physical action (TPR) helps kids instantly feel the correct position. Practice: mother → father → brother — repeat 3 times fast!
