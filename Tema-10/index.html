Learning Objectives

  • 🍎Name common foods and drinks – rice, chicken, bread, fruit, vegetables, water, juice, milk, and more.
  • 👍Express likes and dislikes using I like / I don’t like and He/She likes / doesn’t like.
  • ❓Ask and answer questions about food: Do you like…? Yes, I do. / No, I don’t.
  • 👂Identify foods in context through short listening tasks.
  • 🔊Pronounce plural endings correctly: /s/, /z/, /ɪz/ (fruits, vegetables).

Why This Matters

Food is a universal topic. Whether you are ordering in a restaurant, shopping at the market, or talking about your favourite dishes, you need the right words and grammar. This lesson gives you the tools to talk about what you eat and drink, and what you like or dislike.

💡 Tip — Countable / Uncountable
In English, some foods are countable (e.g., an apple, two apples) and some are uncountable (e.g., rice, water). For uncountable nouns we do not add -s. We use some or a lot of with uncountable nouns: I eat some rice.

Present Simple – Likes & Dislikes

Use the Present Simple to talk about what you like, don’t like, and ask others about their preferences.

Affirmative & Negative
SubjectAffirmativeNegative
II like rice.I don’t like rice.
YouYou like chicken.You don’t like chicken.
He / She / ItShe likes bread.She doesn’t like bread.
We / TheyThey like fruit.They don’t like fruit.
Remember: with he, she, it we add -s in affirmative (likes), but in negative we use doesn’t + base form (like).
Questions & Short answers
QuestionShort answer (+)Short answer (−)
Do you like juice?Yes, I do.No, I don’t.
Does she like vegetables?Yes, she does.No, she doesn’t.
Do they eat meat?Yes, they do.No, they don’t.
Notice: in questions with he/she/it we use does + base form (not likes).
📝 More examples from textbooks
  • • I like chicken, but I don’t like fish. (like / don’t like)
  • • My sister loves fruit. She eats apples and bananas every day. (affirmative with -s)
  • • Do you drink coffee? – No, I don’t. I drink tea. (question and answer)
  • • He doesn’t eat meat, but he eats vegetables. (negative with doesn’t)
  • • We usually have rice and chicken for dinner. (present simple for routines)

Pronunciation – Plural endings

Learn to pronounce the plural -s in words like fruits and vegetables.

/s/
voiceless sounds
fruits cakes drinks
After /p, t, k, f, θ/ the plural -s sounds like /s/.
/z/
voiced sounds
vegetables apples bananas
After /b, d, g, l, m, n, ŋ, r, v, ð, w, j/ the plural -s sounds like /z/.
🎯 The /ɪz/ sound
When a word ends in -s, -sh, -ch, -x, -z we add -es and pronounce it /ɪz/.
Examples: oranges sandwiches boxes (not in our list but good to know).
